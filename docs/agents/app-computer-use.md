Read this before changing how Petal AI uses apps (`petal_ui`, `petal_act`, background acts, the pointer), a run's hidden display, a run's browser, or which screens and apps count as the person's.

# Petal.app: using apps, a display and a browser for each run

In `macos/Sources/Petal/` (the app's overview is in `app-core.md`; filling secrets is in `app-vault.md`):

- `ComputerUse.swift`: Petal AI using apps (`computerUse`; `petal_ui`, `petal_act`, through Assist's socket). `petal_ui` reads the window in front (a note's own window) through Accessibility off the main thread, in 1.5 s at most, and gives each actionable control an id (e12) with its kind, name, value (never a password field's) and frame; the map is kept per chat until the next `petal_ui` or 5 minutes. `petal_act` presses (AXPress, AXPick, AXConfirm), types (the field's AXValue, or keystrokes posted to the app's pid), focuses, scrolls (AXScrollToVisible, or the scroll bar's value), chooses a menu item by path, or presses a key (its menu item when the menus have it, else posted to the pid). Never the real pointer: no warping, no mouse events; what only a real click could do, it says so. Never Petal's own windows either (`UIMap.petalsOwn`: its notes, their editors, its viewer and settings; `petal_ui`, `petal_act` and a vault fill refuse in words, and a helper on a document in Petal's viewer has `petal_view`), and text another program sets in a note's editor through Accessibility is text from outside: it never makes a line Return runs. Each chat is its own caller (its note's id, or `mcp-<pid>` for an AI session outside Petal), so maps and what the person allowed are never shared. **A sticky note works in the background** (`Background.swift`, its pure rules; `BackgroundActing.swift`): an act by a note on its own window (the map `petal_ui` read for the note's place, not `front: true` or another app) goes without the pointer and brings nothing forward. It uses Accessibility only: AXPress, AXPick or AXConfirm (never AXOpen), selecting a row, a field's AXValue (read back: done only when the field reads exactly as asked, else it says it isn't done and never sends), AXScrollToVisible or the scroll bar, a menu item through Accessibility, and focus and keys (posted to that app's pid) only while its app isn't the one in front, and only into the control that reads back as focused; Return likewise. Never anything that uses the clipboard, never a vault fill, never a press that opens another app (`Background.pressOpens`: a link, unless a web address in a browser; a file or anything with a non-web address), and never a menu item or key that opens something (`Background.menuOpens`, `keyOpens`: Open, Open With, Open in…, Reveal, Show in Finder, a new window or tab, in English and Turkish; ⌘O; ⌘↓ in Finder). What it can't do comes back as "needs the window in front" (`Background.Reason`) with nothing done; the AI asks the person, and the note and the AI button say so. Right before each change it reads again whether the person came back to that window or its app (`Background.behind`): before the first change the act is decided again (the pointer and its pause below), after one it stops there. A read of which app or window is in front that fails or times out is unknown (`Background.Known`), never "behind": nothing changes, and the AI is told Petal couldn't tell. A note's act never activates or raises anything, even one decided with its window in front: `bringForward` and `bringWindow` refuse for it (`stayBehind`). When the person has that very window in front the act goes as below, with the pointer, but never within 2 s of their last key, click or scroll there (the hardware's idle times): it waits up to 8 s, then says they're typing. Every change on a note's path (a value set, focus, select-all, each batch of keys, Return, a press, a scroll, a menu item, and a note's vault fill) goes through one gate (`Background.Gate`, `Acting.changeGate`): its guard (the background's `Background.behind`, or in front `Background.interrupt`) runs and the change follows at once, with every read the change needs (where the keyboard is, the page, the window) done before the guard, never between it and the change. In front, the person typing there again or leaving stops it, tells the AI how far it got (what was typed so far; for a fill only whether any of it went in), and never presses Return; leaving before anything changed decides the act again, the background way. Once any change of a background act went through, Petal watches half a second for what it opens: any app that comes to the front (the note's own, or another it opened) gives the person back the app they were using (unless they did something meanwhile), and the AI is told when something opened elsewhere; in the app the person uses, any other window that came over theirs (the note's, or a new one such as Mail's New Message) puts theirs back. An act that changed nothing puts nothing back. A tab's note acts in the background only while its tab is the one showing ("needs its tab showing"). Background acts take nothing from the person's Escape (`Session.shown`); Stop is on the note and in the AI button's preview. Electron apps (T3 Code, Slack, VS Code) get `AXManualAccessibility` once per process before Petal first reads them (`AccessibilityTree`), so their controls exist. Every act, menus and keys too, goes only to the window the last `petal_ui` looked at: it checks the window and control are still where the map said and the window shows what it showed (`UIMap.Fingerprint`: title, selected rows, what has the keyboard), a menu or key needs that window in front of its app, and keys go only once the field reads back as focused. The first act on an app in a reply asks (`Assist.ask`, kind `computer:<bundle id>`; Allow covers the reply, `Agent.turn`; an AI session outside Petal has no turns Petal sees, so its Allow covers one task: the same `task` with under 2 minutes between acts, and after a Stop it asks again), and its card shows only in its own chat (else over the dock); never private apps, password fields or a vault secret, and `petal_ui` shows no vault secret: whole, 8 characters of a long one, or a short one (4 to 7 characters, a PIN) as a word of its own, or a 3- or 4-digit secret (a security code, in any script's digits, found in any script's) anywhere not inside a longer number (`VaultIndex`, hashes kept off the main thread), nor the value of a field named like a password or token. What `petal_act` says of a menu (the items it has, the item chosen) goes through the same redaction, title by title: a Window menu lists window titles. Copying and pasting (keys, menu items, buttons) never moves a vault secret or a password manager's clipboard: all of a text is searched (a rolling hash, so a 1 MB log takes milliseconds), and text over about 1 MB is neither moved nor shown. What hands the selection to another app without the clipboard (`UIMap.sends`: an item under Services, Share or Speech, or in any submenu of the app's own menu, the one after the Apple menu, where Services is in every language; Look Up, Use Selection for Find, a ⌘ key none of the app's menus has, which macOS offers to Services) gets a copy's check of what would go, in front and in the background alike; in the background such an item, chosen or by its key, needs the window in front, as one that opens something does. A Copy or Cut button (or one whose names mention copying) is refused while its window, read again just before, shows a secret or a password, token or API key field; should a secret reach the clipboard anyway, Petal notes only the clipboard's change count before the press and watches it for 0.6 s after on a queue of its own (the reply waits, no other chat's actions do): if the press's own copy (exactly one step on) holds a vault secret or a password manager's private marker and is still that copy, Petal clears the clipboard and never puts anything back; another copy, or the person's own key or click meanwhile, and the clipboard is left alone. Other presses don't touch the clipboard. Anything that might paste never pastes into a password or token field. The window is checked again on the act queue right after the cursor's glide, immediately before the change (fingerprint, which includes the focused control's identity and frame, the control's frame, the menu item). Every change checks its turn's cancel right before it's made, after every blocking read and between each batch of keys: Stop, Escape, steering, a new turn or chat, and turning `computerUse` off or making the app private end it and answer its card Don't; picking another chat ends the one left. Typing straight into a field reads back what changed (its length, the text at the cursor) and never types again when unsure. Maps and turns nobody uses go within a minute, and with their note. The AI may turn `computerUse` off, never on. `FakeCursor`: Petal's own pink pointer in a click-through panel over every Space, gliding (Core Animation) to each control, a ripple on a press, a glass chip saying what it does with Stop; Stop or Escape cancels the rest of the reply's actions. `UIMap.swift`: its pure side (which controls are listed and capped, names, keys, menu paths), checked by `macos/checks/run.sh`.
- `RunDisplay.swift`: a hidden display for each Petal AI run (`runDisplays`). `VirtualDisplay` makes one from CoreGraphics' private classes (`CGVirtualDisplay`, its descriptor, settings and mode), looked up by name at run time and called through the Objective-C runtime, each method only after its arguments were checked to be the ones Petal passes: nothing is linked or declared, so a macOS without them builds, launches and runs as before, and the switch in Settings says it can't. 1440×900 points, at twice the pixels when macOS takes that. It's moved (for this Petal only: `CGBeginDisplayConfiguration`, `CGConfigureDisplayOrigin`, `CGCompleteDisplayConfiguration` with `.forAppOnly`, and nothing else) to where it touches the person's screens only at a corner, the bottom-right one first; never before it has been in macOS's list of online displays with real bounds for a moment (asked sooner, macOS answers 1006), a refusal tried again for up to two seconds, and where it landed read until it's where it was asked; where no corner holds, the display is removed and the run is told, never left beside the person's screen. **Whether a display is there is asked of `CGGetOnlineDisplayList` (`Screens.isOnline`), never `CGDisplayIsOnline`**, which goes on saying yes for the id of a display that's gone (`macos/checks/run.sh` refuses any use of it). A run's display is known by its id and bounds, never by an `NSScreen`: AppKit hears of a new screen in its own time, and a process with no app of its own never does. It has a Desktop of its own (wallpaper, the system's menu bar), so a picture of it shows those. A move is confirmed by what Accessibility reads back; the window server's own list agrees only a moment later. The process owns it: removed when its run ends, when `runDisplays` is turned off, when Petal quits (its windows put on the person's main screen first), and by macOS itself if Petal crashes. `RunDisplays` holds who has one (`RunDisplayPlan`), makes and removes them, opens for a run (`RunDisplays.open`: only a window that wasn't there before is moved, with `Windows.place`; in an app the open itself started, its dialogs too), and keeps which windows are each run's and which apps each run started (`Launched`: the app wasn't running before the run's open). What a run leaves is settled before its display is removed (`RunDisplays.settle`, off the main thread, through `Hands`: the Accessibility and window-server calls, which a check replaces with made-up ones): first the apps it started (`RunDisplayRules.leftover(app:)`: asked to quit with `NSRunningApplication.terminate()` only when the person didn't use it, none of its windows was brought to their screen, and none says it has unsaved changes; given `quitBound` to go), then each window (`RunDisplayRules.leftover`, `holdsNothing`). **How Petal knows the person used an app a run started** (`RunDisplayRules.personUsed`): Petal never brings a run's app forward itself, so an app that comes to the front within a moment of the person's last key, click or scroll was brought there by them, and one that came forward with their hands still is watched, while it's in front, for their first key or click (the hardware's idle times, a quarter of a second apart); Show me, and the run's own `petal_windows` show, make it theirs too. Typing elsewhere at the very moment an app comes forward by itself reads as use: the app is then kept, never the other way round. **Turns on an app** (`AppTurns`, pure): a group has an app while a window of it is on its display that nobody took to the person's screen, while it started the app and nobody used it, or while an open of its own is under way; `RunDisplays.turn` admits every open on a display (`Assist.openOnDisplay`, which holds the app) and every `petal_act` (`ComputerUse.proceed`, which takes nothing), a wait that could never end is refused in words (`circular`), reads never wait, and the quick question's acts never wait either (`RunDisplayRules.takesTurns`: they are the person's own request, in front of them). An app a run started and then opens on the person's screen (`on: "screen"`) is theirs from then on. An app being put away for a run that ended is handed on only once that's done. A run's own unseen app quits, and its own unseen window closes, without a card; another run's app is never quit, hidden or brought forward (`RunDisplayRules.apps`). A run still going is asked once a second (a helper: its token running; a chat: its reply running or waiting for Allow; an outside session: its process alive and heard from in the last ten minutes), and a helper's every ending lets go at once. **`Screens` (`Screens.swift`) is the one predicate for the person's screens**: `Screens.all`, `Screens.main` and `Screens.displays` are every screen but a run's, and nothing else reads `NSScreen.screens`, `NSScreen.main` or `CGGetActiveDisplayList` (the dock's panels, notes, edge tabs, the note pad, tooltips, cards, Petal's pointer, tiling, `keepWindowsClear`, Mission Control's detection, Desktop numbers, which leave a run's Desktop out of the count, and `petal_look`'s picture of the screen); with no run display they are exactly AppKit's. A display's id is a run's from before it comes online until macOS no longer lists it. The dock's previews, window lists and minimizing leave out a window on a run's display (`Windows.windows(of:)`; only Petal AI's tools ask for those, `everywhere`). A run's windows are its own business: `petal_windows` neither lists nor touches another run's, `petal_ui` and `petal_look` refuse to read them, a window on a run's own display never comes forward (focus refuses) and is arranged within it, and `petal_look` with `screenshot: "screen"` shows a run its own display (the picture checked for secrets as any). `RunDisplayPlan.swift` is its pure side, checked by `macos/checks/run.sh`: the plan (join, need, leave, the queue in order, the limit), the corner placement, the person's-screens rule, what an open would do, the leftover rules for a window (`RunDisplayRules.leftover`: closed only when the run opened it bare, did nothing in it, and it reads as holding no document and no unsaved change, or it's a dialog with no document; else brought back; left alone once the person took it) and for an app the run started (quit, or kept), how the person's use is told, and `AppTurns`. `DisplayCheck.swift`: `Petal --display-check`, and its `--full`.
- `RunBrowser.swift`: a browser of its own for each run (`petal_browser`). `BrowserPipe` starts the browser with `posix_spawn` (its own process group, a bare environment, nothing of Petal's open in it but the pipe on descriptors 3 and 4: `--remote-debugging-pipe`, never a port) and matches each reply to its request; the browser ends by itself when the pipe closes. `RunBrowser` is one instance: its tabs, the frames of a page (those in a process of their own too, each asked of its own session), and Petal's own world in each frame (an isolated world the page's scripts can't see or change), where `RunBrowserScript.swift`'s two functions run: `collect` reads a frame into nodes and keeps each control by a number, `act` uses one by its number; what's typed goes in as a value, never as code. A window is made over the pipe at its place on the run's display, in the background, and the browser is ended rather than left on the person's screen if it won't stay there. `RunBrowsers` holds which run has one (by the display's group), starts one when a run first opens a page (its display first), ends it before its display goes (`RunDisplays` tells it at every ending, and at quit), and ends one still starting the same way: a launch is an object of its own (`RunBrowser.Launch`: a cancel flag, the child as soon as it exists, the very display it was started for), so Stop, Pause or the setting going off mid-launch ends the process and removes its profile, and a launch that comes back for a display that is no longer that one is thrown away, never taken over by the run's next start. Each launch runs on a thread of its own. The process is a run's (`RunApps`) from the moment `posix_spawn` returns its pid until it is gone, so no app event for it is ever the person's, not even while it starts. A press that reaches a page (a script run with a gesture, a key) notes the clipboard's count first, and `RunBrowsers` watches it after (`Acting.CopyWatch`, the rule petal_act's Copy buttons go by, for `RunBrowserRules.copyWatch` seconds, the reply not waiting): the press's own copy holding a vault secret or a value Petal typed is cleared, told in the run's next answer and in What Petal AI did. `RunBrowsers` also gives the front back if a run's browser comes forward by itself, and at launch ends and removes what a crash left (found by the profile folder a process was started with, `Application Support/Petal/run-browsers/`; only a folder right inside it by a plain name is ever removed). `RunBrowserPlan.swift` is the pure side, checked by `macos/checks/run.sh`: the pipe's framing, which browser (`RunBrowserRules.choose`: never the one macOS opens the person's https links in, read where the choice is made) and its arguments, what every page is refused (`refused`: the clipboard, sent as `Browser.setPermission` before the first tab; a browser that won't take it is ended), profile folders and the sweep, the address rule, the page's place for the vault (`RunBrowserRules.place`), and `BrowserOutline`, which turns the nodes into text with ids (b12) through `UIMap.redact` and `UIMap.hideJoined`, the very rules `petal_ui` goes by, and checks the whole once more; text and headings share one budget (`textCap`), the controls' lines have their own (`controlsCap`), and what a page does by itself is told a dozen times at most, so no page can make an answer too long to check. `RunBrowserTool.swift` is the tool (`Assist.browser`): the reply's token (`ComputerUse.token`) checked right before each change, the first use asked as kind `browser`, every answer redacted whole before it leaves; and the vault's side: `VaultSurface` is where a vault call's fields are (a window `petal_ui` read, or a page of the run's browser), so `Vault.decide`, the cards, Buy (`Vault.Checkout`, kept under a number that stands for the browser's window, ended when its page moves on) and agents' grants are the same code for both, and only who reads where a field is and who puts the value in differ. Whose a document is (`FrameAt.origin`) is the origin the browser's inspector reports for the frame's own script context (asked by turning its runtime on and off again, nothing run in the page), never the frame tree's `securityOrigin`, which only repeats the address: `RunBrowserRules.effective` makes a sandboxed document, a `data:` page or one the browser said nothing of isolated (`Vault.Place.isolated`: never its address's site to `Vault.decide`, no payment info, nothing saved for the site), and gives a frame with no address of its own (`about:blank`, `srcdoc`, a blob) the site that made it. A vault call's fields are bound when the call comes in (`RunBrowser.Bound`: the element's number in its frame's read, the frame's document and world, the tab and its page's count), and so is an ordinary act's control: an id is never looked up again after a card, and a read made since leaves the bound field with nothing done. In a page the value is set in that very element, in Petal's world, only while its frame still shows the document Petal decided on at the address it decided on. The last checks leave no room for the page's code: right before the write is sent the browser is asked once more, and any word of the page moving on that came in meanwhile (`moves`) gives it up; then, inside the very call that writes, after the field took the cursor (the one step the page can hook) and immediately before the value is set, the document's own address and the tab's are compared with the ones decided on (`placed()` in `RunBrowserScript.act`), and the write is abandoned if they differ. What went in is counted, never read back. `RunApps.swift` is the one list of processes that are a run's, not the person's: `NSWorkspace.personsApplications` and `NSRunningApplication.persons(withBundleIdentifier:)` are what everything that names the person's running apps asks (`macos/checks/run.sh` refuses the raw calls elsewhere); while a run's copy of a browser is going, the dock, `petal_apps` and `RunDisplays.open` open the person's own as a new instance, what an open for a run's display started is never a run's own browser (`RunDisplayRules.started`), and Petal sends that browser no script by its id (`Browsers.access`), since either copy could answer. `BrowserCheck.swift`: `Petal --browser-check`.
