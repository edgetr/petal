Read this before changing Petal AI's chat, its providers and API keys, its tools on the Mac (`petal_look`, `petal_apps`, memory, what Petal AI did), selected-text asks, chats and their sticky notes, routines, quick calls, browsers' tabs or website shortcuts.

# Petal.app: Petal AI, chats and routines

In `macos/Sources/Petal/` (the app's overview is in `app-core.md`):

- `Providers.swift`: the AI apps Petal can use, each the person's own local install and account, as T3 Code uses them: Claude Code and Codex. Everything that differs between them (installer, sign-in, chat arguments, events, quick calls) is one `Provider` case; a new one (Grok, …) is a new case. `APIKeys.swift`: an API key instead of the subscription (see AI is optional in `app-core.md`), its settings card and check, and `APIPricing`.
- `Claude.swift`: the chat panel with `chatProvider`, and the "+" search (its title and field under the list of installed apps, which narrows as the person types; the panel grows and shrinks with what it lists, upward, so the field never moves). An empty chat shows three things to ask and nothing else. Claude Code runs as stream-json sessions, resumed, with Allow, which re-runs with per-part `Bash(...)` rules; Codex runs `codex exec --json`, resumed by thread id, and can't ask mid-turn, so it works in its sandbox (Full access skips it) instead of an Allow card. Either gets Petal's tools (`Petal --mcp`, allowed as `mcp__petal`) and `~/.config/petal/instructions.md` appended to the system prompt. Model and effort come from `chatModel`/`chatEffort` (Claude) or `codexModel`/`codexEffort` (Codex); access from `claudeAccess` for both.
- `Assist.swift`: Petal AI's tools on the Mac (`petal_look`, `petal_apps`, `petal_windows`, `petal_trash`, `petal_remember`, `petal_forget`, `petal_memories`): `Petal --mcp` hands them to the running Petal over the socket (`["assist", tool, json]`, answered later when it asks first), which does them with its own permissions. `petal_look` with `text: true` gives the words of the window it looks at, in reading order (in a browser or web view the page, not the toolbar), read through Accessibility within three seconds by `petal_ui`'s rules (`Reader.read` with `words`: a private app only its name, never a password field, nothing while the vault can't be read, a tab's note only while its tab shows), with only a secret's own part of a sentence hidden (`UIMap.redactWords`); in parts of about 12,000 characters (`part`), `find` for the passages around a word; `WindowText.swift` is its pure side. It is how Petal AI answers about or summarizes a page, a document or a thread; for a video it reads the page and opens the transcript the page offers. A picture asked for without Screen Recording, or apps without Accessibility, makes Petal ask macOS for it (once a minute at most) and tell the AI it just asked. Asking first (`Assist.ask`: the chat's question, or an `.ask` card over the dock; a helper's card waits on its note with no time limit, anyone else's goes after two minutes and is told as unanswered, never as Don't; Full access and an earlier Always allow Petal's own chats and helpers (an owner the panel knows), a routine's own run, and the person's own Claude Code and Codex signed by their makers: `NoteHelp.asker`, `autoGrant`; a card for a caller outside Petal says who asks under that agent's mark, and one for a program Petal can't verify is asked every time, with no Always: `NoteHelp.card`), the last Trash batch to put back, and memory (`~/.config/petal/memory.json`, one for everything, told to the chat in its instructions as things to know, never instructions: each one line of at most 300 characters, 200 at most, `Assist.memoryLine`). `petal_apps` open takes an app by its name, a web address (http or https) or the full path of a document or a folder (`RoutineRules.asked`, then the file as it really is: `NoteTasks.asked`, by Open all's rule): never a script, a program, an installer, an app from a path, a file that points elsewhere or another kind of address, which are refused in words, and in a named app never a file that can be run.
- `AssistMore.swift`: what Petal AI did (`Application Support/Petal/ai-history.json`, Settings › Privacy, with Undo; every line goes through `Assist.did` as a `HistoryLine`: Petal's own words as a literal, whatever it names only as a name, each put through the vault's settled redaction before the line is kept and left out while the vault can't be read), apps it never looks into (`aiNeverLook`: only their name; left out of window titles and screenshots), and keeping the vault's secrets out of everything Petal AI reads (`VaultIndex`, UIMap.SecretIndex).
- `SelectionAsk.swift`: selecting text in any app (a drag, a double or triple click) brings up a small glass "Ask Petal AI" button by it (`selectionAsk`); clicking it quotes the text in the chat. macOS gives no way into other apps' right-click menus beyond Services. Never in apps kept private or password fields; it goes on any click, key or scroll, and after 5 s.
- `Chats.swift`: every chat kept (Application Support/Petal/chats, the last 200) with its session and, for a note, its window (`place`); the panel's Chats list (and `petal chats`) brings one back to read or carry on (a note whose window is gone, as a plain chat).
- `StickyNotes.swift`: the AI chats' notes (the quick question is the one stuck to nothing; `notesOn` is false since Petal Notes replaced `aiNotes`, so no chat sticks anywhere now, and what follows describes the machinery kept for helpers in notes). A note is stuck to a tab, a window or all of an app (`NotePlace.scope`; the chip under the panel's header changes it: "This tab · This window · All of Slack"). Opening the AI reads what's in front off the main thread (the focused window, and in a browser Petal may script and that isn't private, its active tab) and finds the most specific note stuck there (tab, then window, then app), one kept from before (Chats: a window's while its app runs, a Chromium tab's by its id while its browser runs and by its address after it restarts, an app's always), or starts one: stuck to the tab in such a browser, else to the window. Tabs (`NoteTab`): while the browser runs, Chromium browsers by the tab's own id (it survives navigation and being dragged to another window), and Safari, Firefox and Zen by the tab's own button in its window's tab strip: an Accessibility element, the same (CFEqual) for as long as the tab lives, so moving tabs around, navigating and duplicate tabs never pass one tab off as another. The buttons are kept in memory only, behind a number (`TabElements`, `NoteTab.element`, never saved); one gone from its window's strip is let go and its number never comes back. Safari's button is read before and after AppleScript and must agree with the tab's place and count; a tab with no strip (Safari hides it for a window's only tab) can't be told apart, so a note there sticks to its window. Only a Chromium tab after its browser restarted (new ids) is found again by its address (a #fragment counts), and only when it's the one tab of the whole browser with that address, now and when the note last saw it (`NoteTab.shared`, counted with the same address rule as matching), onto a tab with an id, and only when no other note or kept chat fits it as well (`NoteRules.fitting`, on every path: opening the AI, a note following its tab, a chat picked from the list). Safari, Firefox and Zen tabs are found only by their button: after Petal or the browser restarts, their chats wait in Chats, hold no place (`NotePlace.tabComesBack`; two alike never make one let go for the other) and open as plain chats. Opening the AI where Petal can't tell which window is in front (no Accessibility, an app slow to answer) gives the plain chat, never a note it couldn't find again. A note stuck to something else drops its AI's `petal_ui` ids, a read under way and its actions waiting (`ComputerUse.placeChanged`). Notes that fit a tab only by its address and can't be told apart are neither shown nor moved (`NoteRules.fitting`), the notes here and the chats kept from before told apart together, both when the AI is opened and when a note follows its tab by itself, with one candidate per chat (a note here over a kept copy of its chat; a chat picked from the list that sticks again drops its kept link); showing a note's tab selects it by its id (Chromium) or presses its button (Safari, Firefox, Zen). A Safari, Firefox or Zen tab dragged to another window loses its note; a Chromium tab's note whose window closed or browser quit comes back with its tab (Petal keeps listening for that browser while such notes, or chats kept for its tabs, exist). The tab is read with its window (the same window in front before and after, else read again or dropped), and a tab read under way for an app made private meanwhile is dropped. A window Petal couldn't read (one still opening) is never taken for no window (`FrontPlace.none`: the app said it has none in front, as Finder on the desktop). Which of a Chromium browser's scripted windows is a note's Accessibility window is one pure rule (`NoteRules.scriptedWindow`: the one at its frame; among several there, the one whose name or active tab's title starts its title; a browser may mark its scripted name while a tab plays sound, as Aside's " 🔊"), used wherever a tab or its address is read. Browsers say nothing when another tab shows, so a window's title changing (`WindowEvents` `.titleChanged`) and, for two tabs titled alike, the keyboard moving or the tab strip's selection changing in a browser (`.tabSwitched`), both sent only while a tab's note exists or may come back (or a Buy is said in a browser's window, which a tab switch reads again), read the tab again, once it settles. The chat's panel always opens over the dock, in its one place, and can't be dragged (only a paper of Petal Notes is moved by the person: `NoteSpot`). The panel shows one note at a time: what's in front shows its most specific note that was open when the person left or is done, without taking the keyboard, and any other steps away. The pure rules (which notes fit, tabs, the spot's maths) are `NoteLayout.swift`, checked by `macos/checks/run.sh`. Each note has its own `Agent`, up to 4 running at once (the next waits). Its chat's `Petal --mcp` is known as the note's by Petal's own record of the AI process it started for it (`AgentCallers.started`, `Caller.note`; the `PETAL_NOTE` it's given and passes on as `["assist", tool, json, note]` decides nothing), so `petal_look` and `petal_ui` look at the note's window (an app's note: that app's window in front; a tab's: its window, with the note's tab as `noteTab`, and only while that tab is the one its window shows, by its id or button: `ClaudePanel.tabState`, which says its tab shows, another tab shows, or Petal can't tell (only another tab asks the person to switch back; can't tell says what helps and never that), off the main thread, before `petal_look` and `petal_ui` read, after `petal_ui` read, and before every `petal_act`, else refused, never another tab; `front: true` for what's in front). A private app's titles and addresses are never kept, and its tabs aren't read; making an app private takes them out of its notes and the kept chats too, and a chat picked from the list whose window was being looked up meanwhile doesn't stick. Each note's `Agent` keeps `activity` (its latest step, in plain words), `needs` (what it waits for the person for) and `quietly`; `NotePreviews.swift` turns them into lines (`NoteStatus`) for the AI button's preview: hovering the button, as hovering an app, shows each note working, waiting for the person or done and not seen (none: the usage tooltip as before), with a picture of its window by window number and process (ScreenCaptureKit, off the main thread, once a second while open; none for a private app; only a note on all of an app may show another of its app's windows, `NotePicture.pick` and `accepts`), its line and Stop (every note at work or waiting for room, scrolling sideways past four; a note waiting for room has Stop on its own panel too); clicking one brings its window (and tab) forward and opens it there (`go(to:)`), and a note waiting for the person puts a small ✦ on the AI button. A note done while away badges its app's icon (✦) and bounces it once; clicking the icon brings the window (and its tab: a Chromium tab dragged to another window brings the window the browser says holds it, which becomes the note's) and opens the note once its own tab shows; a tab closed while its window stayed open opens its note as a plain chat.
- `TextServices.swift`: "Ask Petal AI" and "Add to Petal AI" on selected text in any app (macOS Services, declared in build.sh's Info.plist): the text becomes a quote chip in the chat, as Petal AI may be told it: each vault secret in it hidden. `Markdown.swift`: one markdown renderer for chat replies and What's New, and `Pages` (`petal_show`), Petal's own glass window for pages the AI makes (kept in Application Support/Petal/pages). A page is only shown (`PageWindow`): its scripts are off, nothing it names on the network is loaded (the viewer's rule list, added before the page is), nothing is kept, the window never goes to another page, and a web link clicked opens in the person's browser; `path` takes only an .html file. A link in a reply or in What's New opens only a web or mail address (`Markdown.inline`).
- `Routines.swift`: routines (`petal_routines`, `petal_routine_save`, `petal_routine_run`, `petal_routine_undo`, `petal_routine_delete`, through Assist's socket): a trigger (a file lands in a folder, an app opens or comes to the front, a time of day) and actions in order (sort files into subfolders, move them, open or hide apps, run a Shortcut), worked out by the chat AI once from the person's words in `~/.config/petal/routines.json`. Only sorting by what files are asks the quick AI (Brain), and its answer is kept per name pattern in `routines-sorted.json`; everything else runs with no AI. `FolderWatch` watches a folder's top (a dispatch source) and hands on a file once settled (size unchanged two seconds; Chrome, Safari and Firefox downloads left to finish). Files are only moved, never deleted, never over another; every run is in `~/Library/Application Support/Petal/routines-log.json`, which puts them back (Settings, Routines). Tidying 10 files or more at once asks first (`Assist.ask`, kind `tidy`). A routine that opens something or runs a Shortcut goes on doing it by itself, so Petal AI's card asks (kind `routine`, which Always covers; the card says what the routine does) before `petal_routine_save` saves or changes one and before `petal_routine_run` runs one; a new name, new words or turning it off isn't asked again. Its open action opens only apps (by bundle id, or an app by its path), http(s) addresses and documents (`RoutineRules.opens`, then the file as it really is by Open all's rule, `NoteTasks.routine`): never a script, a program or another kind of address, which is refused when the routine is saved and left unopened, and said in the history, when it runs. Folders must be in the home folder, not all of it, its Library or a hidden folder: by their paths when a routine is saved or read (one written into `routines.json` with another folder is listed with a line saying so, and never runs or is read), and by where they really are, every link followed, right before each file moves (`RoutineRules.swift`): a subfolder Petal files into is never a link, a link among the files is never moved or handed on, and a folder the routine itself names may be a link the person keeps (`~/Dropbox`) only when it really is in the home folder or in one of macOS's cloud folders (`~/Library/CloudStorage`). A routine saved when the Mac had modes (started by one, or switching to one: `Routine.usesModes`) is kept as written, listed in Settings with a line saying it doesn't run, and never runs. `Petal --routines-check [--ai]` tests it on throwaway folders (`PETAL_ROUTINES` points its files elsewhere).
- `Brain.swift`: quick JSON calls to the chosen AI's quick setting (Claude Haiku; Codex's newest Luna at low effort), one at a time: routines' sorting and helpers in notes. `Browsers.swift`: the front tab's address (AppleScript for Chromium browsers and Safari, Accessibility for Firefox and Zen), and what each app last answered about being scripted, so one app can be asked by itself where it's first needed (`waiting`, `ask`: a note's place menu offers "Let Petal see Safari's tabs…"). `Sites.swift`: website shortcuts and their icons (`~/Library/Caches/Petal/sites`; an icon there is shown, not fetched again: `SiteLoads.swift`), every fetch streamed and bounded (`Sites.get`: an image or an HTML page by its content type before anything is read, an icon at most 512 KB, a page's start 300 KB, 10 seconds).
